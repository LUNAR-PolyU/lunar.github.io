Led by Prof. Siyang Zhong, we are dedicated to the fundamental and applied aeroacoustics research, including the low-altitude aviaiton noise, aircraft nosie and engine noise, etc. We develop physics-based prediction models, perform high-fidelity simulations, and conduct experiments to capture the complex flow physics and acoustic signatures generated aerodynamically.

Our goal is to translate fundamental knowledge into quiet, efficient and sustainable aviation. Particularly, we are also interested in human perception of the noise, especially those from the emerging low-altitude aviation, aiming to bridge fundamental acoustic research with community impact.

Research focus

Advanced computational aeroacoustics (CAA) solver

We specialize in high-order computational aeroacoustics (CAA) to investigate complex turbulent flow physics and acoustic generation around three-dimensional boundaries. By solving the compressible Euler and Navier-Stokes equations with low-dissipation and low-dispersion numerical schemes, our solver framework accurately captures complex aeroacoustic phenomena, including resonant cavity flow noise, turbulent boundary layer interactions, and acoustic generation around airfoils and turbomachinery configurations.

Full-scale aeroacoustic prediction for eVTOL aircraft

We specialize in three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) to resolve complex unsteady flow physics and noise generation around electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) aircraft during flight. Our framework captures intricate mid-scale rotor wake dynamics and aerodynamic interactions between rotors and adjacent airframe components, while quantifying and comparing the drag contributions of the rotors, fuselage, strut, and landing gear under diverse operating conditions. Furthermore, the methodology resolves both near-field aeroacoustic sources and far-field noise propagation to support low-noise airframe design and flight-envelope optimization.

Turbulent inflow interaction with rotor

Utilizing synthetic turbulence within high-fidelity simulations, we study the environmental turbulence interacting with rotors and multi-rotor flying vehicles. The incoming turbulence from the generation region interacts with the rotor wake, causing the tip vortices to lose their symmetry, deform rapidly, and break down, eventually altering the noise features.

Ground effect on rotor aerodynamcis and noise features

High-fidelity computational aeroacoustics studies by solving the Navier-Stokes equations are conducted to investigate the ground effects on rotor aerodynamics and noise. Durign the take-off and landing phases, it is inevtiable that the wakes shed from the rotors will impinge to the group. with high-fidelity turbulence models to capture the complex flow field, followed by acoustic analogy methods to predict far-field noise radiation. The presence of the ground surface introduces significant aerodynamic and acoustic modifications: the rotor wake causes enhanced vortex-ground interactions and generates jet flow near the ground, which not only alters thrust and power characteristics but also intensifies noise generation. Accurate CFD-based aeroacoustic prediction in ground effect is essential for assessing community noise impact and optimizing rotor design for urban air mobility scenarios.

Physics-based prediction models

To accommodate large-scale engineering applications, we establish efficient, physics-based prediction models using aerodynamic theories to predict wake dynamics and rotor loadings, which subsequently serve as the foundation for computing both tonal and broadband noise radiation. Using these physical models, we investigate a wide range of flight states, such as hover and forward flight, while also evaluating coaxial rotor configurations, installation effects, and transitional maneuvers.

Read more ↗

Noise barriers for vertiport

This study presents an optimized design of noise barriers for vertiport environments, employing a frequency-domain acoustic computational method within a surrogate-based optimization process. Low-noise curved noise barriers are specifically designed to attenuate the overall noise emissions generated by UAV operations near the ground, with the curved geometry tailored to enhance interference and diffraction performance across the relevant frequency spectrum. Both on-ground and rooftop vertiport environments are considered, and the proposed low-noise curved noise barriers can surpass the noise reduction performance of taller baseline noise barriers.

Read more ↗

Noise psychoacoustic evaluation

By bridging physical sound generation and propagation with human auditory perception, we aim to advance the scientific understanding of rotor noise mechanisms and their perceptual effects, while developing reliable and computationally efficient tools for noise prediction and assessment.

Principal investigator

Prof. Siyang Zhong

Assistant Professor, Department of Aeronautical and Aviation Engineering, PolyU
Executive Director, COMAC-PolyU Research Institute for Large-Aircraft (中国商飞-香港理工大学大飞机研究院)

BSc (Peking University), MEng (Peking University), PhD (HKUST), MHKIE, MRAeS

Prof. Siyang Zhong is currently an Assistant Professor at AAE, PolyU. Before joining the Department, he worked as a Research Assistant Professor at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) with a junior fellowship at HKUST Jockey Club Institute for Advanced Study. He studied at Peking University for Bachelor's and Master's degrees in Mechanics and Control Theory respectively. He received his PhD Degree in Mechanical Engineering at HKUST in 2018. He is interested in the fundamental studies of aeroacoustics, aerodynamics, and their applications to practical engineering problems. He works in several working groups to develop international (ISO 5305) and national (GB 42590-2023) standards for the unmanned aircraft system. His research is supported by RGC, ITC, NSFC, Guangdong Province, and multiple industrial bodies.

Research interests:

Aeroacoustics & aerodynamics, aircraft noise, sustainable and green aviation of unmanned aircraft systems, high-performance computing, environmental noise and reduction, sports aerodynamics.
