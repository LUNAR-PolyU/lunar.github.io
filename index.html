ABOUT THE GROUP

Research at the intersection of
fluid mechanics and sound.

Our group develops physics-based and computational approaches for aerodynamic and acoustic problems involving highly unsteady flows. We combine theory, numerical simulation, reduced-order modelling, and experiments to connect flow structures with the sound they generate.

Our long-term goal is to enable quieter, more efficient, and more predictable aerial vehicles—from conventional rotors to advanced eVTOL and other unsteady propulsion systems.

01

Unsteady Aerodynamics

Rotor aerodynamics, wake dynamics, blade–wake interaction, and flow-induced loads.

02

Aeroacoustics

Physical mechanisms of tonal and broadband noise and advanced acoustic analogies.

03

Computational Methods

High-fidelity simulation, free-vortex methods, reduced-order models, and acoustic prediction.

04

Next-Generation Flight

Quiet propulsion and aerodynamic technologies for eVTOL and emerging aerial systems.
