Low-altitude UAS noise and annoyance research (LUNAR) group

Led by Prof. Siyang Zhong, LUNAR group is based in the Department of Aeronautical and Aviation Engineering at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. We are dedicated to advancing the understanding and control of unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) aerodynamic noise. We develop physics-based prediction models, perform high-fidelity simulations, and conduct experiments to capture the complex flow physics and acoustic signatures of UAS propulsion systems, ranging from conventional drone to electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) aircraft and urban air mobility (UAM) platforms.

Our goal is to translate fundamental knowledge into actionable insights for quieter, more efficient, and more sustainable low-altitude aerial transportation. Beyond engineering solutions, we also investigate the human perception of rotor noise through annoyance studies, bridging acoustics with community impact. Ultimately, we aim to make future urban air transport not only possible, but also socially acceptable to the communities it serves.

Research interests

Turbulent inflow interaction with rotor

Utilizing synthetic turbulence within high-fidelity simulations, we model the acoustic characteristics of rotors operating in complex urban wind fields. As shown from left to right, incoming turbulence from the generation region interacts with the rotor wake, causing the tip vortices to lose their symmetry, deform rapidly, and break down.

Bi et al. (AIAA paper 2026-3419)

Ground effects

Computational aeroacoustics of rotor drones based on CFD typically involves solving the unsteady N-S equations with high-fidelity turbulence models to capture the complex flow field, followed by acoustic analogy methods to predict far-field noise radiation. The presence of the ground surface introduces significant aerodynamic and acoustic modifications: the rotor wake causes enhanced vortex-ground interactions and generates jet flow near the ground, which not only alters thrust and power characteristics but also intensifies noise generation. Accurate CFD-based aeroacoustic prediction in ground effect is essential for assessing community noise impact and optimizing rotor design for urban air mobility scenarios.

Chen et al. (Aerosp. Sci. Technol. 2026)

Physics-based prediction models

We establish physics-based prediction models by using aerodynamic theories to predict wake dynamics and rotor loadings, which subsequently serve as the foundation for computing both tonal and broadband noise radiation. Using these physical models, our research includes diverse flight states, including hover and forward flight, as well as coaxial rotor configurations, installation effects, and transitional maneuvers.

Zhong et al. (J. Fluid Mech. 2023) Zhong et al. (J. Sound Vib. 2023) Wu et al. (AIAA J. 2025) Zhong et al. (J. Sound Vib. 2025) Wang et al. (AIAA J. 2025) Wang et al. (Aerosp. Sci. Technol. 2026) Wang et al. (J. Sound Vib. 2026) Wang et al. (AIAA paper 2026-3345)

Noise barriers for vertiport

This study presents an optimized design of noise barriers for vertiport environments, employing a frequency-domain acoustic computational method within a surrogate-based optimization process. Low-noise curved noise barriers are specifically designed to attenuate the overall noise emissions generated by UAV operations near the ground, with the curved geometry tailored to enhance interference and diffraction performance across the relevant frequency spectrum. Both on-ground and rooftop vertiport environments are considered, and the proposed low-noise curved noise barriers can surpass the noise reduction performance of taller baseline noise barriers.

Chen et al. (Results Eng. 2026)

Drone noise psychoacoustic evaluation

We bring drone noise into virtual reality. Powered by computational aeroacoustics prediction models and experimental data, we synthesize hybrid tonal and broadband noise sources, incorporate scenario-adapted propagation physics, and deploy spatial audio rendering to deliver interactive, auditory VR environments for psychoacoustic evaluation.

Li et al. (AIAA paper 2026-3315)

Prof. Siyang Zhong

Assistant Professor

Prof. Siyang Zhong is currently an Assistant Professor at AAE, PolyU. Before joining the Department, he worked as a Research Assistant Professor at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) with a junior fellowship at HKUST Jockey Club Institute for Advanced Study. He is interested in the fundamental studies of aeroacoustics, aerodynamics, and their applications to practical engineering problems. He works in several working groups to develop national and international standards for the unmanned aircraft system.

Research interests:
